This is an experimental scratchpad, not the first-1,000 semantic browser. Mapped character examples appear first with their component, initial/final and tone layers. Word hypotheses, characters outside the tranche, and grammar-only experiments have separate sections.

Important: Chinese characters can have multiple senses, and compound words often mean more than the sum of their characters. These prototypes annotate selected meanings, never the Hanzi strokes or radicals themselves. We do not assume a composition is valid just because its emoji look plausible.

What the first test can tell us

An individual pictogram may express some meanings directly. Multi-icon expressions are displayed as emoji only. Relationships and verb participant roles remain machine-readable in the underlying expression tree. No emoji-count limit or uniqueness constraint applies, and separate lexical evidence is still required. Words such as 火车 (train) need their word-level meaning, not a picture of fire followed by a picture of a vehicle.

There is no demand for 100% emoji coverage. When a pictogram expression is ambiguous, its written meaning remains available; the system can also abstain from proposing a pictogram. A future source-backed dataset will attach meaning annotations to reviewed senses and lexical entries, not blindly to every character shape.

Read the experiment and acceptance plan. All component identity, Pinyin and tone coloring remain separate and unchanged. The production semantic Direct/Marker mode remains unavailable until a sense inventory and accessible symbol annotations have passed independent review.
