A visual language research laboratory

Make language visible through independent information layers.

Chromalect develops reversible visual encodings for writing systems, alphabets, sounds, morphemes, words, and linguistic relationships. Mandarin Chinese is the first implemented research profile, not the project limit. Its character prototype supports independent component, pronunciation, and tone layers, while semantic encoding awaits source-backed data. Explore the formal multilingual ontology. The first Mandarin tranche is now source-colored across all 1,000/1,000 characters (measured coverage); the independent Chinese stroke-alignment candidates still await expert visual review.

One layer, two expressions

Direct: style the character or its immediate cell if there is a truthful representation (for example, a tone-colored edge). Marker: an adjacent chip, diagram, or miniature symbol with the same meaning. Both are optional and independently controllable per information channel.

Colors are namespaced by what they encode. The radical/component v0.4 palette does not define pronunciation colors; the prototype assigns independent experimental colors to orthographic Pinyin initials and finals, plus tone shapes and colors. In the absence of verified semantic data the semantic channel is unavailable, not fabricated.

The 2013 list gives ordinal order, not word frequency. Pinyin is citation-level and may have multiple readings for a character. Contextual segmentation, sandhi, etymology, IPA and semantic sense are future data layers—not implied by this demonstration.
